## Fresh-day dative-preposition check — aus/von, nach

4 sentences, testing the aus (enclosed space) vs von (return-from/general
source) distinction flagged as shaky at the end of Session 9.

1. "Ich komme aus dem Haus." ✅ — aus correctly chosen for exiting an enclosed space
2. "Sie kommt vom arbeiten." ❌ → "Sie kommt von der Arbeit." — aus/von choice
   itself correct (von for "returning from"), but confused the VERB arbeiten
   with the NOUN die Arbeit. vom needs a noun object.
3. "Er lebt mit seiner Schwester." ✅
4. "Wir gehen nach Wochenende zum Arzt." ❌ → "Wir gehen nach dem Wochenende
   zum Arzt." — nach dropped its article (same pattern as earlier von/aus
   drops). Two good things in the same sentence: unprompted correct
   time-before-place order, and correctly formed "zum Arzt" (zu+dem, der-word).

**Explained on request (Q15, ANSWERS.md):** why "nach dem Wochenende zum Arzt"
needs BOTH prepositions — nach here means "after" (time), not "to"; zu is the
actual destination preposition. nach also separately means "to" for
articleless places (nach Berlin), which is a different job from its "after" use.

### Retry — both clean

2. "Wir gehen nach dem Termin zur Arzt." — nach dem Termin ✅ (article-drop

   "zur Arzt" ❌ → "zum Arzt" (Arzt is der-word, needs zum not zur — likely

**Net result: the aus/von distinction and the article-dropping habit (von/nach)

## Learner question — Q16: nach vs zu for destinations, full explanation

Asked for the fuller version of the nach/zu split covered briefly in Q15.

**nach** = destinations with NO article (cities/countries: nach Berlin, nach
Deutschland; also directions: nach links/oben; separately "after" for time).
**zu** = destinations that DO take an article (people: zu meiner Mutter;
places: zur Schule, zum Bahnhof; events: zur Arbeit).
Quick test: does the destination normally take der/die/das? No → nach. Yes →
zu (zum for der/das-words, zur for die-words).
Noted for later: some countries take "in" instead (in die Schweiz, in die USA)
— flagged as a known future exception, not to worry about now.

Full answer filed to ANSWERS.md as Q16.
