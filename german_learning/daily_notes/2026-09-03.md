# 2026-09-03, part three — accusative pronouns

**Focus:** Extending the accusative case to personal pronouns

Learner was on a phone with distractions and asked what to do. Recommended
accusative pronouns over dative specifically BECAUSE of the conditions: small closed
set, builds on FUNCTIONAL ground, short sentences, few umlauts to fight a phone
keyboard. Dative deliberately deferred to a focused session — the 2026-09-02
verb-second regression came from material first met under high load, and a shaky
first pass at a whole new case is expensive to undo.

Learner explicitly asked for STRICT typing correction (umlauts, capitalization,
spelling all counted as real errors). Honoured — no leniency applied.

## Teaching approach

Led with the parallel to what is already FUNCTIONAL rather than presenting a table
to memorize:
- der Mann → den Mann ⟶ er → ihn (both change)
- die Frau → die Frau ⟶ sie → sie (neither changes)
- das Buch → das Buch ⟶ es → es (neither changes)

Framing it this way reduced the genuinely new material to five forms (mich, dich,
ihn, uns, euch). Learner took it immediately — every pronoun form was correct in
every sentence today, including ihn.

## Intro set — 5 sentences (4/5)

1. "Ich sehe dich." ✅
2. "Sie sieht mich." ✅ (3rd-person sieht correct here)
3. "Siehst du ihn?" ✅ (du-form correct here — both forms used correctly minutes apart)
4. "Wir haben es." ✅
5. "Er siehst nicht uns." ❌❌ — two errors: (a) siehst for sieht, the recurring
   3rd-person/du pattern; (b) nicht before the pronoun object instead of after
   (EN interference). Correct: "Er sieht uns nicht."
   Note: the pronoun itself (uns) was correct — both errors were in the
   surrounding sentence, not the new material.

## Focused drill — 4 sentences (4/4 clean)

"Er sieht mich." / "Sie hat es." / "Mein Bruder sieht uns." / "Er sieht dich nicht."

All clean, including the nicht placement and the exact "Mein Bruder + sehen" shape
that had failed that same morning.

## KEY FINDING — 3rd-person/du conjugation, load-dependence confirmed

With enough data now, the shape of this error is unambiguous:
- **3 focused drills, 3 clean sweeps** (5/5, 3/3, 4/4) — never failed when
  conjugation is the thing being tested
- **6 failures, all under load** — a new case, new vocab, a new modal structure, a
  new pronoun object. It fails only when something ELSE is new.

Isolated drilling has therefore stopped being diagnostic; it keeps re-confirming
known material. **Action change: no more isolated conjugation drills. Instead every
mixed round from here should quietly include a 3rd-person subject**, so the skill is
exercised under the load where it actually degrades.

Also corrected a tracking gap: the 2026-09-03 morning instance had been noted in
this file and CURRENT_STATE but the ERROR_LOG frequency was never bumped. Count now
accurate at 6.

## Tracker changes

- VOCABULARY.md: mich/dich/ihn/uns/euch + unchanged sie/es/sie/Sie (euch INTRODUCED only)
- ERROR_LOG.md: 3rd-person/du entry rewritten with the load-dependence finding,

Learner wanted to continue, still on a phone. Declined to introduce a fourth new

1. "Meine Schwester sieht ihn." ✅ meine (die-word) + sieht (3rd person) + ihn
2. "Heute kauft mein Vater einen Tisch." ✅ verb-second + kauft + mein (der-word) + einen Tisch
3. "Der Stuhl ist nicht teuer." ✅ Stuhl spelled correctly — this morning's invented
   umlaut did NOT recur
4. "Er hat kein Fenster." ✅ hat + kein with a das-word
5. "Wir konnen dich sehen." ⚠️ können missing its umlaut. Modal + infinitive
   placement correct (können…sehen), dich correct, wir form correct.

## The method change worked (first evidence)

FOUR 3rd-person subjects — sieht, kauft, hat — all correct while genuinely loaded:
new pronouns, new vocabulary, accusative, verb-second and possessives all in play at
once. That is precisely the condition that produced all six previous failures, and
it held. One round is not proof, but it is the right kind of evidence, and it is the
first data point gathered under the new approach rather than from an isolated drill.

Three watch-list items cleared in a single round: conjugation under load, Stuhl
spelling, modal + infinitive placement.

## Umlaut errors split into two distinct patterns

Marked konnen as an error per the learner's strict-typing request, but logged it
SEPARATELY from the invented-umlaut entry, because the two have opposite causes:
- ADDED umlaut (Stühl, Bröt, Äpfel) — a phone keyboard cannot cause this; ü requires
  a deliberate long-press. Genuine knowledge issue about singular vs plural forms.
- OMITTED umlaut (konnen) — exactly what phone-keyboard friction produces.

Merging them would have destroyed the diagnostic value of the invented-umlaut
pattern. Action: retest omitted umlauts on a laptop session; if they vanish when the
keyboard is easy, downgrade to friction rather than knowledge.

# 2026-09-03, part six — second reading passage (harder)

Ran a denser passage immediately, specifically to test the habit flagged in part
five (stopping at the first plausible answer). The trap was NOT telegraphed — the
learner was not told which question was multi-part.

## The passage — "Thomas und seine Familie"

~14 sentences, known vocabulary only, deliberately denser than the first. Embedded:
regnen (impersonal es), kein/keine, modal + infinitive (möchte…kaufen, muss…
arbeiten, kann…schlafen), a separable verb (steht…auf), billig/teuer contrast,
numbers, and an accusative pronoun (es) requiring cross-sentence resolution.

## Result — 4/4, all correct

1. ✅ "Why can't Max buy the dog?" — THE TRAP. The answer is split across two
   sentences with no connector: "er hat kein Geld." / "Er arbeitet nicht." Learner
   supplied BOTH parts and linked them causally ("He has no money as he doesn't
   work") despite the text containing no denn. The part-five habit did NOT recur.
2. ✅ Bread cheap, milk expensive
3. ✅ "es" resolved correctly to das Haus — a cross-sentence pronoun reference
   requiring the learner to track that Haus is a das-word. Notable: es was taught as
   an accusative pronoun roughly an hour earlier and is already working receptively.
4. ✅ Father not sleeping because he has to work — inferred, since "Er muss heute
   arbeiten. Er kann nicht schlafen." has no causal connector at all.

## Findings

- **The stop-at-first-answer habit corrected within the same session**, on an
  un-telegraphed test. One instance each way, so do not over-claim — but the part
  five note should be read alongside this, not on its own.
- **Reading confirmed as this learner's strongest modality.** Two passages, 7/8
  comprehension points, handling causality (marked AND unmarked), pronoun reference
  across sentences, modals, separable verbs and negation — several of which are
  still imperfect in production.
- **New material is entering receptive use fast.** The accusative pronouns taught in
  part three were resolved in running text in part six, same morning.

## Next
Reading is now clearly worth building in as a regular component, not an occasional
extra. Consider gradually increasing passage density, and eventually introducing a
small number of UNKNOWN words in context to practise inference from surrounding
text — the learner's comprehension appears to have headroom for it.
