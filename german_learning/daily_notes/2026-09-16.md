## Past tense — Perfekt introduced

**Structure taught:** haben/sein (conjugated, position 2) + past participle
at the end. Most verbs use haben; motion/change-of-state verbs (gehen,
kommen, fahren) use sein. Participle formation: regular = ge+stem+t,
irregular = often ge+stem(changed)+en (must be learned per-verb, like
English irregular past tense).

1. "Ich habe gespielt." ✅ — regular pattern, clean first try.
2. "Ich bin ins Kino gegangen." ✅ — asked directly why not "gegehen";
   answered: gehen is a strong/irregular verb, stem vowel changes e→a
   (geh- → gang-) in the participle, same idea as English go→went not
   "goed." Correctly used sein (motion verb) and produced the irregular
   participle correctly on the FIRST attempt, unprompted.
3. "Ich haben gegessen." ❌ → "Ich habe gegessen." — ich/haben conjugation
   slip (grabbed the infinitive form instead of habe), NOT a Perfekt-
   structure error. The participle itself (gegessen, irregular e→e stem
   shift) was correct. Isolated retest "Ich habe einen Hund" ✅ confirms
   this was a momentary slip, not a real conjugation gap.
4. "Ich habe gemacht." ✅ — regular pattern, clean.

**Result: 3/4 clean-or-better on a brand-new topic, with the one miss

**Learner asked directly (not filed to DOUBTS.md): how to know which
verbs take sein vs haben.** Answered in full and filed as **Q22,
ANSWERS.md**: sein = motion between two DIFFERENT places (gehen, kommen,
fahren, fliegen) + change of state (aufstehen, sterben) + two memorized
exceptions (sein itself, bleiben). haben = everything else, including
"static" verbs that feel physical but don't involve moving between
places (stehen, sitzen, schlafen).
