## Learner Q7 (DOUBTS.md): Do modal verbs take accusative themselves?

No — modal verbs (können/müssen/möchten) don't determine accusative at all. The
SECOND verb (infinitive at the end) decides, exactly as if the modal weren't
there. Modals are just "helpers" with no object of their own. Full answer filed
to ANSWERS.md as Q7.

Confirmation check: "Ich möchte die Katze sehen." ✅ — correctly left die Katze
unchanged (die-word, accusative invisible), same reasoning as without a modal.

Ties together the whole kein/nicht thread across sessions. kein/keine replaces
ein/eine (or no article) for indefinite/general nouns. nicht negates verbs,
adjectives, whole ideas, AND specific/definite nouns (der/die/das ones). Quick
test: could kein/keine naturally replace the article? Use kein. Otherwise nicht.
Filed to ANSWERS.md as Q8. Confirmation: "Ich kaufe das Buch nicht." ✅

## Learner Q9 (DOUBTS.md): why "nicht den Hund" not "kein den Hund"?

kein/keine and der/die/das articles never combine — mutually exclusive, not
stackable. kein REPLACES the article slot entirely (for indefinite nouns); nicht
doesn't touch the article at all, it's a separate word added near the verb/noun
while der/die/das stays put (for definite nouns). Filed to ANSWERS.md as Q9 —
the mechanical explanation behind Q8's rule.

## Learner Q10 (DOUBTS.md): "Ich sehe den Hund nicht" vs "Ich sehe keine Hund" — both correct?

Not as written. "Ich sehe den Hund nicht" ✅ correct (specific/definite dog).
"Ich sehe keine Hund" ❌ → "keinen Hund" (Hund is der-word, needs keinen not
keine). Once fixed, both valid but mean different things: nicht = "not THE dog"
(specific), kein = "not A dog / no dog at all" (general). Filed to ANSWERS.md as Q10.
