## Q5 — nach vs zu

**a) Rule:** Initial answer used an invented category — "nach is used when
the noun is in infinitive form" — which isn't real German grammar (infinitive
is a verb property; nouns don't have one). Both worked examples were
nonetheless correct (nach Berlin; zu die Stadt self-corrected to zur Stadt),
so the conclusion was right for the wrong reason.

Per the learner's own requested process (surface a gap, self-correct via the
tracker, then retest — rather than being re-taught from scratch), pointed
back to **ANSWERS.md Q16** (answered 2026-09-08) instead of re-explaining.
Learner opened it and restated correctly: **nach = destinations with no
article (place names); zu = destinations that take one (zum Arzt, since der
Arzt).** Correct in their own words.

**b) Examples:** "Ich gehe nach Berlin" ✅. "Ich gehe zu dem Arzt" → self-
corrected to "zum Arzt" while restating the rule ✅.

**c) zum/zur contractions:** ✅ correct — zum = zu+dem (masc/neut dative),
zur = zu+der (fem dative, die Stadt → der Stadt in dative). Reasoning
(zu forces dative) stated correctly.

## Q7 — Verb-second — resolved, 3/3 with one nuance surfaced and fixed

Picked back up in the next chat session (continuing the same day's test):

**(a)** ✅ "Verb always at the second position" — with a useful clarification
volunteered: position/slot, not literally the second word (a whole phrase
can occupy position 1).

**(b)** "Heute esse ich einen Apfel." ✅ — clean demonstration of (a): Heute
takes position 1, esse stays locked in position 2, ich pushed to 3rd.

**(c)** Correctly separated **weil** (sends its OWN clause's verb to the
end: "Weil ich müde bin...") from **denn** (doesn't — behaves like und/
aber/oder, normal verb-second in both halves). What was missing: the
main clause that FOLLOWS a weil-clause still needs verb-second, because
the whole "Weil ich müde bin" clause counts as filling position 1 of the
overall sentence — so "Weil es regnet, **gehe ich**..." not "...ich
gehe...". Isolated check given: **"Weil es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause."**
✅ correct immediately once isolated.

**Result: all three parts correct, with one nuance (subordinate clause
filling position 1) needing a single isolated nudge — not a conceptual
gap, a genuinely subtle extension of the rule.** GRAMMAR.md's verb-second
row updated with this detail; still FUNCTIONAL, not downgraded.
