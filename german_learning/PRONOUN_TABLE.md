# Pronoun Reference — Nominative, Accusative, Dative

Requested 2026-09-05. Quick lookup while the dative is still settling.

---

## The full table

| Nominative (subject) | Accusative (direct object) | Dative (indirect object) |
|---|---|---|
| **ich** — I | **mich** — me | **mir** — (to) me |
| **du** — you (informal) | **dich** — you | **dir** — (to) you |
| **er** — he | **ihn** — him | **ihm** — (to) him |
| **sie** — she | **sie** — her | **ihr** — (to) her |
| **es** — it | **es** — it | **ihm** — (to) it |
| **wir** — we | **uns** — us | **uns** — (to) us |
| **ihr** — you all (informal) | **euch** — you all | **euch** — (to) you all |
| **sie** — they | **sie** — them | **ihnen** — (to) them |
| **Sie** — you (formal) | **Sie** — you | **Ihnen** — (to) you |

---

## What to actually notice

**1. Only four forms are genuinely new in the dative column.**
mir, dir, ihm, ihnen. Everything else you already know from somewhere:
uns and euch are identical in accusative and dative, and es/sie/Sie carry over.

**2. The dangerous overlap — ihr.**
It has three separate jobs, and only context tells them apart:
- **ihr** = "you all" (nominative) — *Ihr habt ein Buch*
- **ihr** = "to her" (dative) — *Ich gebe ihr ein Buch*
- **ihr/ihre** = "her" (possessive) — *Ihre Katze ist müde*

Plus capitalized **Ihr/Ihnen** for formal "you". The verb form usually
disambiguates: *ihr habt* (you all) vs *ihr gibt* (impossible — so it's dative).

**3. er is the only masculine that changes twice.**
er → ihn (acc) → ihm (dat). This mirrors the articles exactly:
der → den → dem. Same shape, so if one is solid the other should follow.

**4. es and sie barely move.**
es stays es in the accusative (like das Buch → das Buch), and sie stays sie.
Only the dative shifts them: es → ihm, sie → ihr.

## Choosing between accusative and dative

Ask what the verb is doing:

| Question | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What is being acted on? | **accusative** | Ich sehe **ihn**. — I see *him*. |
| Who is receiving it? | **dative** | Ich gebe **ihm** ein Buch. — I give *him* a book. |

With a giving/showing/telling verb you often get both at once:

> **Ich gebe ihm den Hund.**
> ihm = dative (the receiver) · den Hund = accusative (the thing given)

**And the second cause of dative:** some prepositions simply demand it,
regardless of any object role — **mit** is the one covered so far.

> **Er spielt mit mir.** — He plays with me.

Here *mir* isn't receiving anything; *mit* just forces dative. (Full list of
dative prepositions is still queued in DOUBTS.md.)

## Possessives take these endings too

Not strictly pronouns, but the same pattern, and it came up on 2026-09-05:

| | der/das-word | die-word |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | mein Vater | meine Mutter |
| Accusative | mein**en** Vater | meine Mutter |
| **Dative** | mein**em** Vater | mein**er** Mutter |

> *Ich gebe **meiner** Mutter die Katze.* — produced correctly, unprompted,
> before this was ever taught.
