# Study Plan — 3 hours/day, from 2026-09-07

**Goal of this plan:** more volume without more speed. The extra hours go into
*depth per concept* — more retrieval, more contexts, more production — not into
covering more grammar per week. The target is intuition (producing correctly
without consciously applying a rule), which comes from repeated retrieval under
varied conditions, not from exposure.

**Set 2026-09-04.** Revisit after two weeks (2026-09-21) against real session data.

---

## Daily shape — three ~1hr blocks, spaced

Spacing across the day is itself a retention mechanism: the gaps force reconsolidation.
Three separated hours beat one three-hour block, and the plan depends on that.

| Block | When | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Retrieval & new material** | Morning | Cold retrieval first, then the day's one new concept |
| **2 — Consolidation** | Afternoon | Practice the morning's concept mixed with old material; reading |
| **3 — Production & input** | Evening | Free writing, listening, speaking; light review of the day |

**Non-negotiable rule:** Block 1 always opens **cold** — retrieval before anything
new, no warm-up, no looking at notes. That's where the real diagnostic signal is.

---

## Block 1 — Retrieval & new material (~60 min)

| Segment | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Cold retrieval | 15 min | Yesterday's material + one spaced item due today. No notes. |
| New concept | 20 min | **One** new grammar point or vocabulary set — never more |
| First production | 15 min | Produce the new thing in 5-8 sentences |
| Error capture | 10 min | Log what broke; if >2 errors, the concept repeats tomorrow |

**Pacing guard:** one new concept per day maximum, regardless of how well it goes.
A clean first attempt is not evidence of retention (proven twice already —
verb-second on 2026-09-02, and the pronoun-selection rule on 2026-09-03).

## Block 2 — Consolidation (~60 min)

| Segment | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed practice | 25 min | Morning's concept + 2-3 older topics in the same sentences |
| Reading | 20 min | A passage using known vocabulary, with 2-3 unknown words to infer |
| Targeted repair | 15 min | Whatever ERROR_LOG flags as ACTIVE |

**Method note carried forward:** mixed practice must *embed* known weak points
rather than drill them in isolation. The 3rd-person/du conjugation error has
passed every isolated drill and failed 8+ times under load — isolation tests the
wrong thing.

## Block 3 — Production & input (~60 min)

| Segment | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Free writing | 20 min | 5-8 sentences, cold, no prompts or word lists |
| Listening | 20 min | Slow German audio; comprehension, not transcription |
| Speaking | 15 min | Read aloud + answer questions aloud (see note below) |
| Day close | 5 min | Update trackers, push |

**Free writing is the core of this block.** It is the single best driver of
intuition because it forces retrieval with zero scaffolding. Topic can be
anything — the day, a plan, a description. Errors here are the most diagnostic
data in the whole plan.

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Mon | New grammar concept |
| Tue | Consolidate Monday + vocabulary set |
| Wed | New grammar concept |
| Thu | Consolidate Wednesday + vocabulary set |
| Fri | Consolidate the week; no new grammar |
| Sat | **Weekly review test** (30-50 questions across everything) + light vocabulary |
| Sun | Light day — reading, listening, free writing only. No new material. |

Two new grammar concepts per week, not five. The other days deepen them.
Sunday's lightness is deliberate: a genuine gap before Monday's cold retrieval
makes Monday's diagnostic honest.

## Listening & speaking — starting from zero

Both are untouched as of 2026-09-04, and both are slow to develop, which is
exactly why they start now rather than after A1 grammar is "done".

**The hard constraint, stated plainly:** this session cannot play audio and
cannot hear you. So neither skill happens *inside* a Claude Code session. Both
run externally, and this session is the **correction and logging layer** — you
bring back what happened, it gets tested, corrected, and written into ERROR_LOG
and VOCABULARY like any other exercise.

### Listening — external audio, report back

**Primary resource: DW's [Nicos Weg A1](https://learngerman.dw.com/en/nicos-weg/c-36519789)**
— free, made by Deutsche Welle with Germany's Federal Employment Agency. ~76
two-minute video episodes at A1, following Nico, a Spaniard newly arrived in
Germany. Each episode has transcripts, vocabulary tools, grammar notes and
interactive exercises. Verified current and free as of 2026-09-04.

**Daily loop (the 20 min in block 3):**
1. Watch one episode **without** the transcript first
2. Watch again **with** the transcript
3. Bring back to the session: what the episode was about, any words you caught
   but didn't know, anything you couldn't parse even *with* the transcript
4. This session then: tests comprehension in German, drills the missed
   vocabulary into VOCABULARY.md, and logs genuine gaps

**Progression tiers** — move up only when the current one is comfortable:
1. Nicos Weg A1 with transcript → 2. Nicos Weg A1 without → 3. Nicos Weg A2 →
4. *Slow German* podcast (Annik Rubens) → 5. DW's *Langsam gesprochene
Nachrichten*

**Note on that last one:** DW's slowly-spoken news is **B2-level content**, not
a beginner resource — real journalism read slowly, not simplified. It is a
much later tier, listed here so the ladder is complete, not as a near-term
target. Don't reach for it early; it will feel like failure when it isn't.

### Speaking — Claude app voice mode, report back

Claude Code has no voice. Real spoken practice with correction happens in the
**Claude app's voice mode** (mobile or desktop, not this CLI).

**A ready-made context prompt was written on 2026-09-01** — it lists exactly
what's been learned so the voice session doesn't jump ahead. See that day's
daily note; update it as new material lands, since it goes stale.

**Loop:** speak in the app → note what broke (words you couldn't retrieve,
corrections it gave you, anything you avoided saying because you didn't know
how) → report it back here → it gets logged and drilled like any other error.

**Secondary, free, every day:** read your typed answers aloud during normal
exercises. This builds articulation and rhythm. Be aware of its limit — this
session can correct the German you type but has no way to hear or correct
pronunciation.

## What "intuitive" requires, concretely

Intuition is not a separate activity — it is the *result* of these conditions,
all of which this plan is built around:

1. **Retrieval, not review.** Producing from memory beats re-reading, always.
2. **Spacing over massing.** Three separated hours, and the spaced-repetition
   schedule in CURRENT_STATE, do more than the same time bunched together.
3. **Varied contexts.** The same rule met in a translation, a reading passage,
   free writing, and speech generalizes; met only in drills, it doesn't.
4. **Production under load.** Errors appear when attention is elsewhere — which
   is why mixed practice matters more than isolated drills.
5. **Enough repetition after "getting it".** Understanding a rule and using it
   automatically are weeks apart. This is what the extra hours buy.

---

## Guards against the failure mode this plan invites

More hours makes it tempting to cover more. The specific risks and their guards:

- **Risk: covering more grammar per week.** → Guard: max 2 new grammar concepts
  per week, hard limit, regardless of how well things go.
- **Risk: treating a clean first attempt as mastery.** → Guard: nothing is
  promoted past LEARNING without holding across a real overnight gap. Already
  burned twice.
- **Risk: fatigue in block 3 producing junk data.** → Guard: if block 3 quality
  drops noticeably, cut it short. A bad session pollutes the error log with
  fatigue artefacts that look like knowledge gaps.
- **Risk: vocabulary volume outrunning usage.** → Guard: every new word must
  appear in a produced sentence the same day, and again in that week's review.
