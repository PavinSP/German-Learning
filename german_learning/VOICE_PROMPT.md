# Voice Mode Context Prompt

Paste the block below into the **Claude app's voice mode** (mobile or desktop —
not Claude Code) at the start of a spoken practice session. It tells that session
exactly what has been learned so it doesn't reach past it.

**Included free with a Claude Pro plan.** No OpenAI key or extra cost needed.

**Keep this updated** — it goes stale every time new grammar or vocabulary lands.

## The prompt — copy from here

I'm learning German from scratch and I'm at a late-beginner A1 level. Please be my
spoken conversation partner.

How I want you to run this:
- Speak slowly and clearly, in short sentences.
- Stay inside the vocabulary and grammar I list below. If you need a word I don't
  know, use it but say the English immediately after.
- Correct my mistakes out loud, briefly, then keep the conversation going. Don't
  let errors pass, but don't stop to lecture either.
- Ask me questions rather than talking at me — I need to produce, not just listen.
- If I go silent, I'm probably stuck retrieving a word. Wait a moment, then offer it.

Grammar I know and can mostly use:
- sein and haben, present tense, all persons
- Regular present-tense verbs, plus these stem-changing ones: essen (isst),
  lesen (liest), schlafen (schläft), sehen (sieht), tragen (trägt), geben (gibt),
  nehmen, sprechen
- Definite and indefinite articles (der/die/das, ein/eine), plurals
- Negation: nicht and kein/keine, including when to use which
- Accusative case (der→den, ein→einen, kein→keinen) and accusative pronouns
  (mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, uns, euch)
- Dative case, recently learned and still shaky (dem/der/dem, einem/einer) and
  dative pronouns (mir, dir, ihm, ihr, uns)
- The preposition mit (takes dative)
- Possessives: mein, dein, sein, ihr, and formal Ihr
- Modal verbs: können, müssen, möchten — with the second verb at the end
- Separable verbs, e.g. aufstehen ("Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf")
- Verb-second word order, including when a sentence starts with a time word
  ("Morgen arbeitet mein Vater")
- Conjunctions: und, aber, oder, denn
- W-questions: wer, was, wo, wann, warum, wie

Vocabulary areas I have: greetings, family, numbers 1–20, food and drink, daily
routine and telling time, weather, shopping, house and rooms, clothing and
colours, hobbies, and frequency words (immer, oft, manchmal, selten, nie).

What I have NOT learned yet — please avoid these:
- Any past tense (no Perfekt, no Präteritum)
- Future tense with werden
- Adjective endings before nouns (say "Das T-Shirt ist grün", not "ein grünes T-Shirt")
- Subordinate clauses with weil, dass, wenn — I only know denn for "because"
- Genitive case
- Reflexive verbs

Known weak spots to watch for and correct me on:
- I sometimes use the du verb form when the subject is er/sie/es — for example
  saying "er siehst" instead of "er sieht". This happens most when I'm
  concentrating on something else.
- The verb geben trips me up: it should be gibt for er/sie/es, and I've said
  geht or gebt instead.
- Dative is new, so I may use accusative endings where dative belongs.

Please start by asking me my name and how I'm doing, then move into everyday
topics — my day, my family, food, the weather.
